The three options you have today

All three make you pay for the quiet months

There is nothing wrong with any of them when the roadmap is full. The problem is that AEM roadmaps are almost never full twelve months a year.

Option 1 · In-house team

You keep the knowledge, which matters more in AEM than in most stacks. You also pay the salary in every quiet month — and senior AEM people are scarce enough that letting one go is close to irreversible.

12 months paid for 6 months of work

Option 2 · Full-time contractors

Faster to start and easier to stop. The arithmetic is identical while they are engaged: the invoice is the same in a heavy month and a dead one. And when they go, the context goes with them.

Same invoice, none of the retention

Option 3 · Partner retainer

Capacity on call, which is the right idea. Then a monthly minimum whether the work shows up or not, and unused hours that quietly expire at the end of the period.

Where this does not work

Three cases where we are the wrong answer

You genuinely have full-time AEM work. If the calculator puts you above the break-even line month after month, a team of your own is cheaper and better. We will say that on the call rather than after the first invoice.

You need someone in the room. On-site presence, a security clearance, or a contract that requires a local legal entity — that is not us, and pretending otherwise wastes a quarter of your time and ours.

You want the cheapest hour on the market. There are offshore rates well below ours. They are real. What they do not include is a working day that overlaps yours and an architect who has done this migration before.
